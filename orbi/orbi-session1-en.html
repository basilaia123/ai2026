🏛️ The 4 Pillars of Engineering Prompting & Anti-Hallucination (ORBI Benchmark)

Strict Standard

When dealing with construction and contractual documentation, hallucinations are unacceptable. We enforce a 4-pillar verifiable methodology:

1️⃣ Few-Shot Prompting (The Gold Standard)

Feed the model 1-2 approved historical ORBI letters or BOQ formats before giving the instruction. The model mirrors the exact structure, tone, and legal precision at 99%+ fidelity.

2️⃣ Chain-of-Thought (CoT) & Self-Refine

Instruct the model to think step-by-step: "First extract penalty clauses, verify against Decree 41, run a self-audit on calculations, and only then draft the formal letter."

3️⃣ XML Tag Structuring

Segregate prompt layers cleanly: <context>, <rules>, <document_excerpts>, <output_format>. Eliminates prompt bleed and instruction confusion.

4️⃣ Strict Fallback Rule (Anti-Hallucination Barrier)

Hard requirement: "If any clause, dimension, or unit rate is not explicitly present in the provided files or Decree 41, output [DATA NOT FOUND - CONFIRMATION REQUIRED] and never invent."

🧠 Preventing "Lost in the Middle" across 500-Page Specifications:

When processing massive design packages, place your most critical statutory constraints at the very beginning of the prompt and repeat them at the end so attention weights remain sharp.

03

Engineering Prompting: C.R.E.A.T.E. & P.A.R.T.S. Frameworks

15 Minutes

A generic prompt ("Write a warning letter to the contractor") produces vague, legally useless output. Engineering prompting demands disciplined structure:

The C.R.E.A.T.E. Framework:

  • C - Context: ORBI asset, current project phase, and verified delay specifics.
  • R - Role: Senior PM, Chief QA/QC Supervisor, or Principal Architect.
  • E - Examples (Few-Shot): Benchmark excerpts from approved historical letters.
  • A - Ask: Precise deliverable (e.g., 3-point formal notice with liquidated damages).
  • T - Tone: Authoritative, legally fortified, assertive, composed.
  • E - Execution: Exact layout (Markdown table, numbered clauses, signature block).

The P.A.R.T.S. Framework:

  • P - Purpose: Primary outcome: contractor delivers a recovery Catch-up plan in 72h.
  • A - Action: Analyze root causes, quantify penalties, and set escalation gates.
  • R - Role: Director of Engineering, ORBI Group.
  • T - Topic: 14-day schedule slippage across HVAC installation package.
  • S - Style & Scope: Single-page formal notice citing specific contract clauses.
❌ Weak, Intuitive Prompt: 
Write a warning letter to the contractor telling them they are late and we will fine them if they don't fix it.

Result: Emotional, non-specific prose lacking contractual enforceability, citations, or binding cure periods.

✅ Engineering C.R.E.A.T.E. Prompt: 
[Role]: Lead Project Manager, ORBI Group
[Context]: Central Park Towers, HVAC subcontractor 14 days behind schedule.
[Ask]: Draft formal contractual claim via DEFUSE framework.
[Constraint]: Mandate Catch-up schedule in 72h. Liquidated damages: 0.1%/day.
[Format]: Formal corporate letter with official metadata and signature block.

Result: Legally robust, itemized document ready for executive review and immediate delivery.
